# Local buses: network, journeys and service restrictions

The **Local** workspace displays the public Islamabad/Rawalpindi bus catalogue, available stop locations and route geometry. It is an offline imported network, not a live vehicle tracker or a copy of Safar's code/data. Source research and licensing are recorded in [TRANSIT_SOURCES.md](../data-sources/TRANSIT_SOURCES.md); coverage gaps and reproducible public-data import are in [TRANSIT_DATA.md](../data-sources/TRANSIT_DATA.md). Source archives stay ignored. Only the normalized public derivative in `assets/transit/islamabad.json.gz` is bundled.

## Coverage and provenance

The bundled network has **37 routes / 1,210 source stop records / 67 direction patterns / 2,064 timetable trips**. Its final dataset version ends `-roads-8d109199`. Of those directions, 49 use road-aligned estimates, 14 retain supplied shapes and four retain mapped Orange geometry. Estimates connect **954/974 consecutive stop pairs**; **20 remain explicit gaps**. No live vehicle positions, operator confirmations or invented gap links are supplied. [Reproduction and gaps](../data-sources/TRANSIT_ROAD_ALIGNMENT.md).

## What the planner actually knows

- Every coordinate is GeoJSON `[longitude, latitude]`. Calendar/service days use **Asia/Karachi**, including Sunday `0` and GTFS times above `24:00` belonging to the previous service day.
- A route is a public line; a pattern is one ordered direction/variant. Stop order, boarding/alighting restrictions, source gaps and restricted-access patterns are preserved.
- **Provided shape** means the source supplies a continuous line. It does not prove a currently running bus or an operator-approved diversion. **Mapped corridor** means a documented OSM road/station derivation, without invented GPS or timetable observations. **Road-aligned estimate** means a precomputed, bus-access-aware path through ordered source anchors; actual operator streets can differ. **Stops only** supplies locations and order without a supported street path; the map retains those stop dots and hides their off-road connection chords.
- Unusable coordinates are excluded and represented as a gap. Known stops on either side remain visible, but the planner never rides across the missing segment. Reference-only lines such as FR-12 may list published stop names without placing or routing through guessed coordinates.
- Published GTFS trip times are used only on an applicable calendar date, respecting added/removed service exceptions. Intermediate times can be **source-model estimates**; a scheduled result is not a live prediction. A known expired timetable produces no fabricated scheduled service.
- A pattern with **no timetable at all** may produce an explicitly untimed structural connection: `timing: unknown`, null arrival/duration and null vehicle departure times. It is not silently assigned a bus frequency or a made-up journey ETA. Partial known schedule information does not turn an unknown connection into a timed promise.

## Journey search and limits

`POST /api/transit/plan` accepts strict `{ origin: Place, destination: Place, departureAt: ISO }`. The existing car planner and its road/access validation are unchanged. Local-bus journeys contain ordered walking and riding legs, intermediate stops and the selected route pattern.

Search considers up to **32 nearby source stop IDs**, including distinct IDs at the same physical platform, access/egress walks up to **1 km**, walking transfers up to **250 m**, a **60 second** connection buffer, at most **two transfers**, **90 minutes** maximum scheduled wait and **four hours** maximum internal journey horizon. Walking distance is a straight-line estimate at **1.25 m/s**, not street-validated pedestrian guidance. Overpasses, controlled crossings, inaccessible stations and private entrances may make a geometrically nearby stop unreachable. No walking line is advertised as turn-by-turn navigation.

A bounded heap search retains two labels per stop/ride-count/mode/timing class, with a maximum **1,800 expansions / 2.5 seconds**. Validated completed connections are collected when generated, before unrelated branch expansions can consume the budget. A small uncertainty tie-breaker keeps untimed metro/Orange patterns searchable alongside scheduled feeders; final presentation still prefers scheduled results. Geometry intersections and ride slices are memoized **inside one request's immutable snapshot**, while each trip's temporal checks remain separate. Caches never cross closure revisions/requests. Additional eligible connections can exist beyond these limits; an explicit warning accompanies a truncated search. This is a small MVP planner, not an exhaustive transit optimization engine. It returns up to three distinct pattern sequences; getting off the same bus a stop earlier is not presented as a separate route. Fewer genuine alternatives are returned honestly. Internal untimed ranking uses distance only to compare candidates; its heuristic is never exposed as a vehicle ETA.

Pickup/drop-off restrictions are enforced, including source stops requiring advance arrangements. Restricted-access patterns such as FR-04B are visible for reference but excluded from ordinary journeys. An empty or corrupt dataset returns an unavailable network/plan instead of fixtures. Origin/destination outside the bounded mapped access area returns an explicit unavailable reason.

Patterns can additionally carry a documented **`routingUnavailableReason`**. They remain grey/uncertain for source review but cannot produce journey legs; this does not assert that the operator suspended the service. The official comparison currently applies it to two RLRT03 variants whose IJP/Potohar order contradicts the full Metro corridor. Full RLRT01 is preserved. A route's scoped `officialReview` disclosure carries dates/reference links and distinguishes textual agreement, differences, historical references and unestablished route-level confirmation. Journeys using other difference-reviewed routes retain a reference warning. Old CDA PDFs do not silently replace current mapped sequences, and neither a matching authority record nor the review label certifies current operation or field coordinates. [Evidence and refresh](../data-sources/TRANSIT_DATA.md).

## Local map presentation

Selecting a computed journey focuses its actual ride legs and stops. Unrelated patterns become thin translucent grey context (2.6 px / 18% opacity), with smaller faded stop dots; the used directions retain a subdued colour outside the ridden section (4.5 px / 45%). The exact ride remains a solid 8.8 px line with its white casing. Switching options updates the same retained map sources. An old catalogue selection cannot highlight unused stops during journey focus. **Bus routes** restores the full catalogue; **Your journey** restores the chosen option, and clicking a map route returns to its route details, including when that same catalogue pattern was selected earlier. No new network download, road geometry or plan request is needed for these display switches.

Muted context is separate from `availability`: fading an available line does not label it suspended. Uncertain/suspended directions and ride legs stay grey even when emphasized. A stale snapshot removes journey focus/guidance and greys the network until freshness checks succeed. The existing revision/time/access rules remain in force.

The selected stable pattern-sequence option also survives minute timetable refreshes when it remains in the current result, including reordered alternatives. Pending requests retain the previous valid display. If that option is no longer returned, selection falls back to a current eligible result rather than retaining invalid guidance. Returning from Map/Profile restores the journey tab alongside its retained map focus after the ordinary re-entry revision check.

Local defaults to the restored pale **Minimal** basemap with a separate browser preference; the driving map retains its own profile style. [Map-style behavior](../guides/MAP_STYLES.md). Ordinary mapped bus lines are **4.5 px / 96% opacity**, selected lines **7.8 px / 100%**, and journey ride legs **8.8 px**. All bus strokes are solid. A narrow, subtle dark casing replaces the broad white network casing. Unresolved bus sections remain gaps, and raw stop-only chords are hidden; estimated walking connectors remain dashed without a continuous white underlay. Stop circles remain visible at city overview (around zoom 11–12), shrink/fade at regional zooms, and emphasize selected/ridden stops. Red road overlays remain above transit lines and stops.

`src/lib/transitColors.ts` assigns one presentation colour per route ID across directions, map lines, route cards and ride badges. It builds weighted display-neighbour relationships within **250 m**, sampling supplied segments at about 120 m and deduplicating points in 60 m cells; physical stops also establish proximity. Separate geometry segments and documented gaps are respected. Deterministic saturation/weighted-degree ordering chooses among ten non-red colours using OKLab separation; shared corridors receive priority over brief crossings. Grey (`#697781`) remains reserved for uncertain/suspended/stale service. White route-badge text meets 4.5:1 against the normal and grey palettes. Availability labels, selected stops and explicit missing-section disclosures retain information beyond colour.

The finite palette cannot make every route at a dense interchange unique. Some weak neighbouring contacts reuse a colour, and exactly shared geometry can still overpaint; selecting a route brings it forward. No fictitious lateral offset or road segment is introduced. Tests check contrasting colours for the committed snapshot's strongest nearby contacts, without promising a universal graph-colouring guarantee. Publisher colours remain in the canonical source asset; presentation colour assignment itself does not alter source data, timetables, geometry or routing/access rules. The separate road-alignment derivative changes geometry and the dataset version as documented above.

Colour schemes are cached by immutable network arrays/version, with an exact-content bounded cache for refreshed arrays. Clock/status/selection changes do not rebuild the spatial graph, and zoom does not calculate colours. Equivalent wrapper objects and unrelated UI updates do not rebuild overlay features or resend map sources. The current public network has **18,343 displayed vertices**; transfer/asset provenance is in [road-alignment coverage](../data-sources/TRANSIT_ROAD_ALIGNMENT.md). Device, cold-start, HTTP and rendering latency can differ.

## Road closures versus bus suspension

**Red remains reserved for road closures.** Bus route colors use other hues. At the selected time, a known published/approved road restriction intersecting a route paints that route direction grey as **uncertain**, rather than asserting that a bus operator has suspended it. Pending/private, draft, rejected, cancelled and ended road reports do not affect the public bus network. Future restrictions do not grey today's network.

The planner checks the ridden part of a bus line at its expected traversal time. For scheduled trips it uses each source stop's arrival/departure interval, including intermediate dwell, rather than stretching the total trip duration uniformly. Unknown-time journeys conservatively consider the four-hour search horizon. Affected legs are excluded; a fixed bus service is never arbitrarily rerouted onto streets as if it were a car.

Provided shapes, mapped corridors and inferred road alignments establish **geometric closure risk**, not a confirmed operator action or independently verified road clearance. Estimated streets may differ from the actual service. Exact inferred stop-pair paths govern closure checks and journey slices; unresolved sections cannot be ridden across. The retained `stops-only` contract has `closureAssessment: unavailable` and no rendered bus chord, but the current bundled derivative has no remaining stops-only patterns. Unaffected portions of a partially restricted line can remain eligible for a journey even while the complete network direction is grey.

A reviewed **bus service notice** is separate from a road closure. It identifies a known route, optional direction and optional ordered stop segment, with explicit start/end instants and a source/reference. At its active time, it marks the relevant direction **suspended**. Network geometry remains conservatively grey for the affected direction; the UI labels section-only notices **Partial suspension**, retaining **Service suspended** when any active whole-line/direction notice applies. Journeys exclude only overlapping ridden segments; unrelated directions and unaffected intervals stay eligible. Published future notices are available for planned-time evaluation. Cancelled notices stop affecting planning.

An ordered partial notice matches only patterns where its start stop precedes its end stop. The reverse direction is not suspended merely because it contains both stops; this now matches the planner's existing ordered-segment evaluation. A whole-route notice without a direction still affects both directions. Canonical short drawn blockades use their actual validated road footprint for intersection checks, with the same approved/public and active-time requirements as continuous road closures.

Champ/Gemini extraction can prepare a **private service-suspension proposal**, including conservative line/ordered-stop matching. Every source-generated service notice requires explicit administrator approval; missing/ambiguous line scope or times stay for manual input. Verified contributors/admins can also publish structured notices manually. Source posts, guessed operator reroutes and headlines never automatically become confirmed transit operation. [Source review details](../operations/AGENT_TRANSIT_REVIEW.md).

## Fare references

Journey options and actual boarding legs can include dated adult base-fare references. A complete total exists only when every boarding has an established reference; partial/unknown remains explicit and an unknown ride is never free by default. A known subtotal is not a complete ticket quote. Separate boardings are charged separately unless an integrated policy has been established; concessions/discounts are not inferred from the user profile.

Fare evidence does not validate service operation, grant access, fill a timetable gap or change route ranking. Untimed journeys remain untimed even when their fare is known. There is no fare polling/payment service: maintain the reviewed reference module deliberately as tariffs change. [Source evidence, route-family rules, dates and fare fields](TRANSIT_FARES.md).

## HTTP API

| Endpoint | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/transit/network?at=ISO` | Public network, ordered stops/patterns, active-time statuses, published service notices, sources/limits; excludes bulk timetable trips and personal data. Missing `at` uses current time. |
| `GET /api/transit/revision` | Lightweight `{version,eventRevision,disruptionRevision}` for Local-only freshness polling. |
| `POST /api/transit/plan` | Public strict origin/destination/departure request; ordered journeys or explicit unavailability. |
| `GET /api/transit/disruptions` | Public published notices; verified/admin reviewers can also see cancelled notices. |
| `POST /api/transit/disruptions` | Verified contributor/admin publishes or records a cancelled notice; server supplies author, ID, revision and timestamps. |
| `PUT /api/transit/disruptions/:id` | Strict complete draft plus positive `revision`; verified contributors/admins can edit or cancel. Stale revisions return `409`; agent corrections retain an override. |

Only the public network response negotiates asynchronous gzip, with `Vary: Accept-Encoding` and `Cache-Control: no-store`; identity and `gzip;q=0` remain supported. It omits backend-only `roadSegments` and stop distances and removes an exactly duplicate single display section, retaining separate sections, gap indexes, estimate provenance and access/source-conflict metadata. Exact ridden paths arrive in the plan response. This is transfer compression, not permission to reuse stale availability.

Unknown routes/directions, one-sided or reversed stop segments, invalid time zones/end intervals, arbitrary client author fields and non-HTTP(S) source URLs are rejected. The API enforces permissions independently of the UI. Ordinary users and guests can inspect/plan, but cannot publish service notices.

Plans capture the public road-event revision and service-notice revision, then recheck both before responding. A changed restriction returns **409**, preventing a mixed-revision recommendation. The UI separately rejects stale responses and invalidates results after relevant changes. These freshness checks do not promise instant external incident reporting.

## Storage, privacy and release

Service notices are a bounded shared `transit:disruptions` metadata document with a global revision and at most **100 retained records**. SQLite uses `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`; Firestore uses an SDK transaction, so competing writes cannot silently overwrite a newer notice. The Firestore `transit:` prefix points to the shared app root, never a user's private profile. Existing author identity is retained on an edit; no API accepts a forged author. This implementation does not upload personal GPS, copy local SQLite into cloud storage or grant new account permissions.

The bundled catalogue needs no runtime network-import poll, paid mapping key or access to a user account. Actual source/Gemini jobs and public service notices have separate authorization/operational configuration. Data refreshes must rerun/review the public importer and update its coverage manifest; a current fetch date is not proof of current operator service. See [implementation status](../IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md) for release scope.

## Verification

Focused synthetic planner/API regressions cover schedules and exception days, GTFS midnight rollover, pickup/drop-off restrictions, actual transfers/co-located IDs, minimum transfer time, bounded walks, unknown/expired timetable behavior, source gaps/restricted patterns, geometric closure risk at traversal/dwell time, partial/directional service suspensions, source validation, permission enforcement, atomic conflicting edits, cancellation, future-time statuses and revision-change rejection. Synthetic fixtures live only in tests and are not runtime network fallbacks.

The committed derivative also has directed OSM edge proof, exact stop-pair slicing, source-gap/access preservation, missing-shape unavailability, gzip transport and retained-map/loading regression coverage. A full-network Orange regression prevents scheduled feeder branches from starving an eligible untimed direct service. Executed runs belong in [implementation status](../IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md). Tests do not establish operator field accuracy, real walking access or currently running vehicles.
